# Finite cylinder expression boundaries

`boundaryFiniteCylinderExpression3` constructs a regularized boundary for a finite expression tree whose leaves reference factory-owned filled finite cylinders. All operand walls and caps are partitioned against every radial support and both axial planes of every operand. The input tree is snapshotted with validated indices, operations and cycle rejection.

For every open face cell, each operand supplies a constant material location. Inside and outside labels give equal membership on the two infinitesimal sides of the source face. A boundary label requires a coincident support: circular cylinder walls share the outward radial convention; coplanar caps use the exact sign of the dot product of outward rational normals. Consequently the two Boolean membership vectors are determined without choosing an epsilon or evaluating approximate spatial offsets.

Evaluate the complete expression on both vectors. Equal results discard the patch; differing results retain it, with its outward orientation from material to empty space. Coincident patches are emitted by the lowest-index contributing primitive only. Every other operand boundary on an open cell is checked for coincident support; point/edge contacts do not justify whole-face coincidence. Pairwise complete arrangements provide coverage of these representative patches, including source-domain limits.

The selected curves are sewn as exact native conics and cylinder SSI edges. Every edge must have at least two coedges with balanced directions; otherwise construction rejects. `manifold` reports closed oriented incidence and does not claim generic imported-shell embedding certification. Artificial subdivisions remain. The result is a constructive boundary; common Body adoption, persistent expression recipes, regularized point location and region containment are not yet supplied by this module.

Executable regressions cover a three-operand nested difference/union with inward cavity walls, three crossing axes with SSI edges, coincident XOR cancellation, opposed cap removal, reversed-axis duplicates, independent exact nearby material checks, factory ownership, invalid leaf indices and cyclic input. General oblique and multi-operand contact arrangements still require additional regressions before broad use. This is not a Lean proof or whole-program verification.
