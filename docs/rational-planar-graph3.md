# Preserve the abscissa field on rational planar line graphs

A planar arrangement's fallback segment graph previously calculated an ordinate from its abscissa, then passed both through the generic two-parameter plane map. The coordinates were exact, but their dependence could be lost. Later ellipse support checks could consequently construct unnecessarily large independent-field resultants.

When both actual segment endpoints have rational coordinates in the validated arrangement chart and their abscissae differ, their line is exactly `y = m*x + k`. The world chart `o + u*x + v*y` is therefore `o + v*k + (u + v*m)*x`. The graph point now uses an actual affine 2D point `(x,m*x+k)` through the validated plane-map factory. This yields that genuine world affine construction with the actual abscissa as its single algebraic source, and also retains the actual fixed-abscissa line parallel to the frame's second vector. Coordinate defining degrees do not exceed that source's degree. No floating-point fit, tolerance or caller-provided line flag is used.

The slope/intercept derive from the actual endpoint coordinates obtained through the partial inverse chart. The line constants are retained only within the arrangement call under its immutable canonical curve objects. Known event reuse remains first. Vertical or nonrational chart segments retain the existing complete fallback; this change does not infer a rational line from approximate coordinates or discard general algebraic input.

A regression checks the synthetic line point `(sqrt(2), sqrt(2), 0)` on `y=x` at an unrelated circle event. It requires actual affine construction provenance, exact plane support, the coordinate-degree bound, and the fixed-abscissa line construction with equal local x and zero local y at its base. The case fails on the preceding runtime because affine correlation is absent and passes on the corrected runtime. It does not assert a timing threshold or claim the old coordinates were numerically wrong. All 35 connected chart/partition/endpoint cases pass. A follow-up exposed and repaired loss of the fixed-abscissa construction in a direct-world-affine implementation: routing through the actual affine plane map preserves both identities rather than adding a public certificate flag.
